## Slice boundary

**Slice 0.1.0 (in):** the mod's own `twilightforest:twilight_portal` block (its declarations
written here; its art re-authored in the licence-clean pass, below), its
frame/decoration/activator tags, and the activation rule exactly as upstream writes it — a
framed water pool (≥ 4 blocks, ≤ 64), each pool block on a sturdy floor, ringed by `PORTAL_EDGE`
blocks with a `PORTAL_DECO` block above, lit when a diamond is dropped into it, the catalyst
consumed and lightning summoned.

**Slice 0.2.0 (in):** the level `twilightforest:twilight_forest` with the jar's dimension type, a
`BiomeSource` reproducing `twilightforest:twilight_biomes` over the jar's `biome_grid`, 14 carried
biomes, the blocks its surface rule places, and the `/twilightforest dimension` readout — §“The
dimension, slice 1”.

**Out of both:** the terrain, the other 8 biomes, decoration, carvers, structures, mobs, bosses,
progression, the client effects and the portal's travel (the gap list has file:line).

## The dimension (gap list, re-measured at the SDK pin)

At campaign pin `1d63e0a` this was `blocked(worldgen-api)`, with file:line evidence in
[`FINDINGS.md`](FINDINGS.md) §3 and the in-game proof in
[`ports/_shared/evidence/twilightforest/dimension-probe.log`](../_shared/evidence/twilightforest/dimension-probe.log)
(`execute in twilightforest:twilight_forest …` refused with `Unknown dimension`, while the same
line for `minecraft:the_nether` moves the player and a block placed there reads back).

**The SDK unblocks the API.** Measured in the SDK at `0b14f8a`
(`.horizon/sdk/api/crates/horizon-mod-vocab/src/parts/dimensions.rs`,
`src/host/services.rs`, `src/world/dimension.rs`):

- `Registrar::register_dimension(name, DimensionSettings)` — “every world then has the level
  `mymod:pocket`, shaped and generated by `settings`”. `DimensionSettings` carries `min_y`,
  `height`, `logical_height`, `has_skylight`, `has_ceiling`, `generator`, `noise_settings`,
  `biome`, `biome_source` and `mod_biome_source`.
- `register_chunk_generator::<T>(name, generator)` and
  `register_biome_source::<T>(name, source)` — the two code hooks the jar's dimension needs
  (`terrain_data: twilightforest:biome_grid` + `twilightforest:twilight_biomes`).
- `EntityRef::teleport_to_level(host, dimension, pos, rotation)` — the portal's travel, which
  `getPortalDestination` needs.
- `RuntimeLevels::open/unload/delete` — mod-owned levels at run time (cap 64), not what a static
  dimension needs but what a “pocket” would use.

So the remaining work is **content, not API**: 21 biomes as a `BiomeSource`, the hollow-hill/giant-
tree terrain as a `ChunkGenerator`, the decorators and structures (which a mod chunk generator does
not get for free), the progression, the bosses and entities, the `neighborChanged` reversion of an
unsupported portal back to water, the custom portal renderer (`PortalOverlay` swirl,
`render_type`), the `is_one_way` return portal, the advancement lock
(`portalLockingAdvancement`), the safety pre-check (`isSafeAround`) and the 60-tick portal wait.

**Slice 1 (2026-11-18) took that re-slice** and is recorded below. The record changes from
`blocked(worldgen-api)` to **`ported-partial`: the level and the biome grid are the jar's, the
terrain is a recorded gap**, and nothing in the dimension is faked.

### What the biome source reproduces

`WorldUtil.getOverworldSeed()` and `LazyAreaContext(25, salt)` are the jar's seeds, and every layer
is reproduced value for value in pure Rust (`src/grid.rs`): the LCG step and `mixSeed`, the layer
random, `KeyBiomesLayer`'s `java.util.Random` jitter (offsets 1–2 over 8x8 regions, a 0–2 rotation
of the four landmarks), the companion, zoom (normal and fuzzy), stabilize, border, seam, smooth and
filtered layers with the jar's own salts, and `TerrainColumn.getBiome`'s nearest-elevation rule
with its tie to the lower key. **The placement is exact for all 22 biomes**; only the *set of biomes
this package carries* is bounded.

The proof is a **second implementation**: `ports/_shared/evidence/twilightforest/oracle/TFForestGridOracle.java`
is a standalone Java program written from the same upstream classes, using `java.util.Random` and
`long` arithmetic. `grid-golden.txt` is its output — 60 seeded cell/elevation samples across four
seeds (including seeds ±2^63-ish and cells at ±65,536) plus a 4,096-cell digest and distribution —
and `src/grid.rs`'s tests assert it line for line, digest included.

**The stated simplification.** The slice carries 14 of the 22 biomes: the jar's surface rule names
nine of them (`highlands`, `thornlands`, `final_plateau`, `snowy_forest`, `glacier`, `lake`,
`stream`, `swamp`, `fire_swamp`), the grid's four key biomes and its `clearing` landmark and
`thornlands` edge are among them, and `forest`, `underground` and `highlands_underground` complete
the columns. A grid answer outside that set answers the nearest carried biome **of its own family**
(`runtime`-adjacent table in `src/grid.rs::carried`): `dark_forest` → `dark_forest_center` (the
companion pair the layer stack itself derives), and the seven remaining members of the jar's
`random_biomes` lists (`dense_forest`, `dense_mushroom_forest`, `enchanted_forest`,
`firefly_forest`, `mushroom_forest`, `oak_savannah`, `spooky_forest`) → `forest`. No biome id is
invented, and a reader can see exactly which eight the package does not have.

### Deviations of this slice

1. **The carried biomes are the jar's biome *definitions*, not its decoration.** `carvers`,
   `features` and the natural spawns are empty in all 14: the carver is a configured carver the
   slice does not carry, every `features` entry is a placed feature of the mod's own (trees, ores,
   plants, `druid_hut`…), and every spawner names a mod entity type. A user standing in one of the
   biomes sees its colours, its temperature and its rain, and no plants or animals.
2. **The two client grass-colour modifiers are dropped.** `dark_forest_center` and `swamp` declare
   `grass_color_modifier: twilightforest:*`, a Java-registered type; 26.3's `effects` takes only
   vanilla's (`swamp`, `dark_forest`, `none`). Their `foliage_color`/`grass_color` numbers are
   carried, so the tint is the flat colour upstream's modifier varies.
3. **The surface blocks are the stone template.** `twilightforest:deadrock` and its two variants
   are declared on `minecraft:stone` (`item: true`); the jar's own hardness, sounds and map colour
   are Java code and are not guessed. The three textures are **this port's own**, drawn
   deterministically by `tools/assemble_surface_blocks.py`: the upstream repository's
   `ASSET_LICENSE` is CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0, so no upstream art is carried into new content.
4. **The noise settings' router is a decodable stand-in.** The jar's `surface_rule` is carried node
   for node as a 26.3 `material_rule`, and the settings' numbers (`min_y`, `height`,
   `default_block`, `default_fluid`, `sea_level: 0`, no aquifers) are the jar's — but the eight
   `noise_router` roots are constants, because the two Java density-function types they would name
   cannot be decoded (`blocked(...)` above). Nothing reads them at this pin (the chunk generator
   asks for no vanilla stage), so the difference is invisible today and is exactly what a later
   terrain slice replaces.
5. **No travel.** See the gap list: the rule is pure and tested, the teleport is not wired.
6. **`day_cycle: "none"`.** The jar's fixed time is a value; 26.3's dimension type names timelines
   instead. `has_fixed_time: true` keeps the sky still; how dark the sky is at 13000 is not
   expressible here.

### The gaps, with file:line

- **`blocked(density-function type: twilightforest:biome_driven_terrain)`** and
  **`blocked(twilightforest:biome_driven_noise)`** —
  `data/twilightforest/worldgen/density_function/raw_biome_terrain.json:2` and
  `raw_biome_noise.json:2` (both also carry `terrain_source: twilightforest:biome_grid`), reached
  from `forested_terrain.json:4`/`:30` and used by `noise_settings/twilight_noise_gen.json`'s
  `final_density`/`initial_density_without_jaggedness`. Horizon's density registry loads **all
  entries atomically** and implements the bundled function kinds
  (`ports/_shared/pins/horizon-1d63e0a/crates/horizon-world/src/worldgen/density.rs:60-63,120-140`;
  `docs/world-generation.md`), so the jar's graph cannot be carried as data at this pin.
- **`blocked(terrain)`** — because of the two types above: the level has no noise terrain. The
  slice registers `twilightforest:twilight_terrain`, a `ChunkGenerator` whose `build_terrain`
  writes nothing and which asks for no vanilla stage, so the level is empty and honest instead of
  a vanilla Overworld or a synthesised heightmap. The moment the two density types are expressible
  (or a slice writes the jar's `biome_driven_*` maths in Rust), the `noise_settings` and the
  material rule carried here are what the terrain is painted with.
- **`blocked(fixed_time value)`** — `dimension_type/twilight_forest_type.json:5` (`13000`): the SDK
  has `has_fixed_time` but no field for the value (`world/dimension.rs:129-130`); the port sets
  `has_fixed_time: true` and `day_cycle: "none"` so the clock does not move.
- **`blocked(effects)`** — `dimension_type/twilight_forest_type.json:4` (`effects:
  twilightforest:renderer`): `DimensionSettings` has no effects id (`world/dimension.rs:192-250`);
  the sky stays the Overworld's.
- **Not ported:** the other 8 biomes; the jar's carver `twilightforest:tf_caves` (its
  `configured_carver/` entry is data but the slice asks for no carvers); every `features` entry
  of the 14 carried biomes (decoration, trees, ores, plants — the mod's own placed features are
  not carried); the biomes' mod-entity spawners; structures, mobs, bosses, progression and the
  client effects (`twilightforest:wandering_firefly` particle, the music sound event, the two
  `grass_color_modifier`s).
- **The portal's travel is decided but not wired.** `grid::travel_target` answers
  overworld ↔ `twilightforest:twilight_forest` and is unit-tested; the teleport itself is not
  called because the destination is empty at this pin — a player sent there would fall out of the
  world. One `EntityRef::teleport_to_level` call (`hooks_tests.rs:781-787`) once the terrain slice
  lands.
- **`blocked(harness)`** — the in-game leg. No `horizon-launcher` exists here to build a game
  carrying the package, so nothing about the dimension is *measured in game*: the level's
  registration, the biome source's answers and the readout are proved by `cargo test`,
  `cargo check`, `check` and `fits` only. `tools/e2e.sh` carries the legs (leg 2) ready to run.

## Deviations

1. **No catalyst owner check.** Upstream requires `entityItem.getOwner() == player`. The SDK's
   `ItemEntityRef::owner(host)` **does** exist now (API 42's `native_item_entities` exposed no
   owner), so this deviation is closable — it is kept for this slice because the 0.1.0 e2e drops
   its diamond with `ctl drop`, and a scripted drop need not carry the thrower, so the check would
   have to be re-proved in game. **Later slice:** add it and re-run the e2e.
   A user would notice a shared pool lighting from someone else's diamond.
2. **The activator is matched by item id**, not by the jar's tag chain: the jar's
   `#twilightforest:portal/activator` is `#c:gems/diamond`, and 26.3's base game ships
   no `c:` tags, so the reference can never match there. The runtime resolves it to
   the tag's only vanilla member (`runtime::ACTIVATORS`), and the content declares
   both tags so a pack can still add members. See “Upstream defects fixed”.
3. **Pool cells are water or a one-way portal block**, where upstream compares
   against the start block's own state: a pool mixing water and one-way portal blocks
   would be accepted here. No slice code creates a one-way portal.
4. **Visuals.** Upstream draws the swirl with a custom renderer (`PortalOverlay`,
   `render_type: translucent`, one 13/16 plane). The port declares no model-level
   render type and the renderer is not carried, so the block draws a full cube in the
   texture upstream's own model names (`minecraft:block/nether_portal`). Light 11,
   no collision, glass sound and the 0–13/16 outline are carried. Since the licence-clean pass
   the barrier's own model declares no `render_type` either (this pin reads none —
   `docs/resource-packs.md`; upstream's was NeoForge's), and the regenerated barrier texture is
   opaque, so the layer choice changes nothing a player sees.
5. **No `neighborChanged` reversion**: a lit portal whose frame is broken stays a
   portal block here; upstream turns it back into water. A later slice can take it
   from `Hooks::neighbor_changed` (the catalogue has it).
6. **No advancement lock, no safety pre-check, no 60-tick portal wait** — all three
   are either about travel (which has no destination) or about a config the port
   does not read.
7. **Config defaults are constants**: `maxPortalSize = 64`, every player may light a
   portal (`portalCreationPermission = 0`), the 20-tick cadence, the 32-block scan.
   Upstream reads them from `TFConfig`; a config page is not part of this slice.
8. **The mod's own plants are not carried** (mushgloom, mayapple, fiddlehead,
   fallen/giant leaves, moss patch, clover patch, steeleaf block, hardened dark
   leaves): their entries are dropped from the carried decoration tag, which keeps
   the vanilla members and the vanilla tags (`#minecraft:flowers`, `#minecraft:leaves`,
   `#minecraft:saplings`, `#minecraft:crops`).
9. **The old glue's log lines are gone** (`Twilight Forest: lit a 16-block portal …`,
   the per-failure `describe` dump). They were `log::info!` in a mixin module; an SDK
   crate has no `log` dependency, and adding `log = "0.4"` makes it a *registry*
   dependency the launcher wants pinned with `horizon-launcher native-lock .` — which
   is not installed here, so it cannot be proved acceptable. This port ships with no
   dependency of its own on purpose. The assertions are `ctl block` reads; a failure
   now says *that* a pool did not light, not why. Re-add the traces when a dependency
   can be pinned (see “What a huge port's migration looks like”, §7).

## Licence-clean pass (2026-11-18)

**Why.** The jar's `neoforge.mods.toml` declares the **code** LGPL-2.1, and the campaign recorded
rank 1 on that basis — but `TeamTwilight/twilightforest`'s own `ASSET_LICENSE` is
**CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0**. A content port carries assets, so as shipped the package was rank 4 and
deliberately local-only (ARCHITECTURE.md §8, ruling 2026-11-18). The way out is the MineColonies
precedent: **the code may be ported, the art must be regenerated.** The Rust was already the
campaign's clean-room rewrite; the only encumbrance was seven carried files.

**Measured first.** `tools/check_provenance.py --check` against the delivered 0.2.0 package found
**7 of 61** packaged files byte-identical to a jar entry — the whole job, and nothing else:

| file (before) | what replaced it |
|---|---|
| `assets/twilightforest/textures/block/portal_barrier.png` (14,392 B, the jar's 16×512 animation) | `tools/gen_portal_texture.py`: a new 16×512 RGBA texture, 32 frames of a three-armed spiral drawn from a fixed palette (`DEEP`/`MID`/`BRIGHT`/`HIGHLIGHT`), a centre glow and a radial rim. No randomness, no upstream read; 13,792 B |
| `…/portal_barrier.png.mcmeta` (the jar's `frametime: 2, interpolate: true`) | the same generator: an explicit 32-frame list, `frametime: 2`, **no `interpolate`** (this pin steps frames — `docs/resource-packs.md`), 376 B |
| `assets/twilightforest/blockstates/twilight_portal.json` | `tools/assemble_content.py`: the same two-variant multipart (the portal's model always, the barrier's when `is_one_way` is true), our own key order (`when` before `apply`) |
| `assets/twilightforest/models/block/twilight_portal_barrier.json` | `tools/assemble_content.py`: the same geometry intent — two horizontal planes at 11.5 and 13.5, no ambient occlusion, both faces textured — with our own uv, a `particle` texture and no unread `render_type` |
| `data/twilightforest/tags/block/portal/edge.json` | `tools/assemble_content.py`: membership unchanged (`#minecraft:dirt`, `dirt_path`, `farmland`), sorted |
| `…/portal/fluid.json` | `tools/assemble_content.py`: `minecraft:water`, sorted |
| `…/portal/generated_decoration.json` | `tools/assemble_content.py`: the **vanilla** plants, sorted; the three mod plants it named (`mushgloom`, `mayapple`, `fiddlehead`) are **dropped**, because this port does not carry them |

**The gate.** `tools/check_provenance.py` opens the jar and the built `.hmod`, compares the sha256
of every packaged file with every jar entry (by content, so a copy under another name is caught)
and exits 1 listing the offenders; `--check` runs the assertion only. It also writes
`PROVENANCE.md`, one line per packaged file with its bytes and its provenance — *authored*,
*generated (`tool`)* or *rewritten-from-semantics* — which is the audit trail `LICENSE-NOTICE.md`
points at. It was written **first** and run before the pass: 7 offenders, exit 1. Its
fail-capability is reproduced in `evidence/twilightforest/licence-provenance-red.log`, which
restores the seven carried files into a copy of the delivered package and gets the same seven back
(same paths, same sizes); after the pass the same command reports **61 packaged files, 0 identical
to any of the jar's 10,437 entries** (green, `licence-provenance.log`).

**Around the seven.** Three things had to follow them:

* `tools/assemble_content.py` no longer reads the jar at all — it was the 0.1.0 carry script, so
  rerunning it would have reintroduced the upstream bytes. It now writes the portal's blockstate,
  barrier model, its own full-cube model, the four portal tags, the lang file, the block
  declaration, the loot table and both activator tags, and calls the texture generator, so one
  command reproduces the whole portal's content.
* The **lang tip for the portal is the port's own sentence** now. The 0.1.0 file embedded the

## Upstream defects fixed

**The activator tag can never match at 26.3.** The jar's
`data/twilightforest/tags/item/portal/activator.json` is `{"values": ["#c:gems/diamond"]}`;
the `c:` convention namespace is supplied by NeoForge/Fabric packs, and 26.3's base
game ships no `c:` tags at all (`data/c/` absent from the prepared 26.3 resources), so
as written the portal would never light. Evidence: the jar's tag file, and the absence
of `c:` tags in the prepared resources.

What the port does instead: the runtime matches the resolved member
(`runtime::ACTIVATORS = ["minecraft:diamond"]`), *and* the content declares the tag in
both places a pack may look — `data/c/tags/item/gems/diamond.json` and
`data/twilightforest/tags/item/portal/activator.json`, whose values carry both
`#c:gems/diamond` and `minecraft:diamond`. A user sees the portal light with a diamond
as upstream intends; a pack that adds a member to either tag is still honoured by
whatever reads the tags, while the runtime's own list is the documented resolution.

Worth an upstream report? Yes, a small one: “the activator tag names a convention tag
the target game does not ship”. Not filed — the orchestrator decides.

### Traps this migration hit

1. **`tools/audit/Cargo.toml` refused the package** — `error: unexpected package path:
   tools/audit/Cargo.toml`. The old source-audit helper sat inside the port; any `Cargo.toml`
   under the port root is read as a package path. Moved to the retired evidence folder.
2. **`cargo horizon-mod build` does not compile.** A syntax error in `src/lib.rs` still produced
   a `.hmod` (`note: … the file does not parse as Rust`); a *type* error produces a package that
   the registry check passes and the game then fails to build. `cargo check`/`cargo test` are the
   compile gate; `build`+`check` are the packaging gate. Say this in every brief.
3. **`cargo horizon-mod check` with no file checks wasm** and fails with
   `can't find crate for 'core' … wasm32-unknown-unknown target may not be installed` (imfast
   trap 3). Always `check <file.hmod>`.
4. **The host traits are the game's side; a mod calls handles.** This cost the first three
   compile rounds: `host.player_level`, `host.player_dimension`, `host.player_permission_level`,
   `host.entity_position`, `host.item_key_id` do not exist for a mod — they are `*Backend`/hidden
   methods. The mod-facing forms are `player.level(host)`, `player.dimension(host)`,
   `player.permission_level(host)`, `entity.position(host)`, `item.id(host)`,
   `level.block(host, pos)`, `item.stack(host)`. The `host.*` methods that *are* public are the
   write/serve side (`host.summon`, `host.block_in_tag`, `host.block_key`, `host.block_state_id`,
   `host.item_entity`) and are reachable on `&mut dyn ServerHost` **without importing** the area
   traits, because they are `ServerHost`'s supertraits — so `use mod_api::prelude::*` is not needed
   in a mod at all (it is unused-import warned), only in a game or a test double.
5. **Two clippy errors the old module never met** (the old helpers were injected into the game and
   never clippy'd on their own): `x % 20 != 0` → `!x.is_multiple_of(20)`, and
   `permission < PORTAL_CREATION_PERMISSION` / `configured >= LEVEL_ADMINS` on constants with the
   extreme values 0 and 2 → `clippy::absurd_extreme_comparisons`. The gate and the radius became
   `fn scan_range(permission: u8, configured: u8) -> Option<f64>`: same logic, both sides
   parameters, so the configured defaults stay visible and the lint cannot fire.
6. **A dependency is a packaging decision.** `log = "0.4"` compiles (`cargo check` green) and is
   already in the SDK lock, but it makes the crate depend on a registry package the launcher wants
   pinned with `horizon-launcher native-lock .` — unprovable without the launcher. The port ships
   with none and records the lost diagnostics (deviation 9).
7. **`data/c/…` is carried on purpose** and `cargo horizon-mod check` accepts it: it is the
   convention tag the upstream defect needs (above), declared beside the mod's own namespace.
8. **`--dev-mod` disappeared from the e2e.** Content now registers because the package is
   installed, so the scenario passes no flag; the old “embedded content blocked” control leg (a
   `.horizon-cache` file) has no meaning and was replaced by a **no-mod control** on the plain
   game of the same pin.

## What a huge port's migration looks like

*Read this with `imfast/notes.md` §“Migration to the SDK format”, which is the recipe. This section
says what changes when the port has many helpers and a body of content — it is the reference the
other large ports (`gtceu`, `mekanismgenerators`, `immersiveengineering`, `botania`,
`modern_industrialization`, `integrateddynamics`, …) copy.*

**Size of this one, for calibration:** 2 retired code helpers + 1 support module (1 hook,
5 glue functions), 14 carried data/model/texture files, 1 carried block; the package is 23 entries,
44 KB, and `uses.json` names **86** surface items. A `deep` port with 10–40 hooks scales the
*glue* linearly and the *package* with its content, and nothing else.

### What generalises (copy these)

1. **Migrate in this order and do not interleave:** scaffold → `cmp` `runtime.rs` into `src/` →
   move `assets/`+`data/` to the package root → delete the retired layout **before the first
   build** (any surviving `mixins/`, `.hnmod` or nested `Cargo.toml` refuses the package) → write
   the glue → compile → package → `check`/`fits`.
2. **`runtime.rs` is the line that makes it a migration.** `cp`, then `cmp`, then `cargo test`
   **green before any glue**. Every number the port proved stays proved; the glue is the only new
   risk, and it is a risk about *plumbing*, not about behaviour.
3. **`lib.rs` registers, a module glues.** With N hooks: one `impl Hooks` with N one-line methods,
   and the bodies in `src/<moment>.rs` (`portal.rs` here). Split the glue by *moment*, never one
   file per hook — a huge port would otherwise recreate the retired `one helper per file` shape
   for no benefit. `pub mod runtime;` and `pub mod portal;` (a private module dead-codes every
   `pub` item in it).
4. **The helper→hook table is the whole review.** Write it before the code: every old helper, its
   typed replacement, kept/gap. It is what makes a 40-hook migration reviewable, and it is what
   `uses.json` then confirms item by item (`cargo horizon-mod check <file> --uses`).
5. **Handles, not host areas** (trap 4). Read the `impl <Handle>` blocks in
   `generated/mod_api_generated_host.rs`, not the `host/<area>.rs` declarations: the area files are
   the *game's* side, and half of what a huge port needs (`player_level`, `entity_position`,
   `item_key_id`, `level_block_state`) is only there under a `*Backend` name a mod cannot call.
6. **`HostSide` decides the half.** Any `side = "both"` hook (player ticks, level ticks, block
   changes) gives `HostSide`; `let HostSide::Server(host) = host else { return; };` is the whole
   client half, and it is honest — the client cannot write the world.
7. **State on the struct, not in statics.** `Mod { … : Mutex<…> }` with `&self` hook methods. A
   huge port has several such maps (caches, counters, per-level state); they all live here, which
   is also where a future config would live.
8. **Content stops being a trick.** The `content_root`/embedded-`.hmod` pattern (§1.0 of
   `AGENT_MOD_STRUCTURE.md`) existed because the registry took one file per project. The flat
   package carries code **and** content, so a “both halves” port is finally listable and the port
   deletes the injection, the `.hmod`-in-`data/`, and the write-out code. Check the carried tree
   against the jar with the port's own carry script — here `assemble_content.py`, run in a scratch
   copy and `diff -r`'d against the port — it is cheap and it will catch a half-moved content tree,
   which is the realistic huge-port mistake.
9. **Verify in two gates, because they prove different things.** `cargo check`/`test`/`clippy`
   prove the glue compiles against the *pinned API*; `build`→`check`→`fits` prove the *package* is
   well-formed and pinned. Neither runs the code. A behaviour claim still needs the game.
10. **Reproducibility is a two-line check worth doing:** remove `.horizon/` and `target/`, rebuild,
    compare the sha256 (here `9f29089d…` both times) — it also proves the SDK fetch is
    self-sufficient for the next agent.

### What is one-off for this port (do not copy)

- **The portal rule and its measured numbers** (14 tests: the 2×2/4-block minimum, the L shape, the
  full-cube sturdy floor, the 64-block inclusive budget, the horizontal-only flood fill). Nothing
  here generalises past “the runtime is where the numbers live”.
- **The dimension re-measurement.** Every `deep` port whose notes say `blocked(worldgen-api)` must
  re-measure it at the SDK pin: `register_dimension` + `register_chunk_generator` +
  `register_biome_source` + `EntityRef::teleport_to_level` now exist, so “no fourth level” is no
  longer true. Re-read the block reason before scheduling the re-slice; do not copy this port's old
  wording.
- **The `c:` activator fix** (upstream defect) and the 26.3 content shapes (singular
  `loot_table` condition, `data/<ns>/horizon/block/…`) are twilightforest-specific.
- **The no-mod e2e control.** `NO_MOD_GAME` must be a game of the *same* revision without the mod;
  nothing publishes one at the SDK pin yet (`ports/_shared/bin/` has `1d63e0a5` and `41f822b6`), so
  the leg fails until a plain `0b14f8a` build is made. Do not point it at another revision — that
  would compare two engines and prove nothing.
- **The diagnostic loss (deviation 9)** is a consequence of this box lacking `horizon-launcher`;
  a port that can pin a dependency should keep its traces.

## Undone in this slice

- **The in-game leg.** `tools/e2e.sh` is retargeted but **not re-run**: a native package is
  compiled into the game by the launcher, and `horizon-launcher` is not installed here
  (`which horizon-launcher` → nothing; `cargo horizon-mod run` answers *“code mods run through the
  launcher's native build: horizon-launcher native-build . --trust-native-code”*). The 0.1.0 run
  still stands for the *rule* (byte-identical, 14/14) and for the mapped behaviour, but **the new
  glue's in-game pass is unproved** and must be run before the port is called `ported`:

  ```sh
  horizon-launcher native-build ports/twilightforest --instance <instance> --trust-native-code
  GAME=<that horizon-client> NO_MOD_GAME=<a 0b14f8a game without the mod> tools/e2e.sh
  SELFTEST=1 GAME=… tools/e2e.sh        # must exit non-zero
  python3 ports/tools/check-e2e-logs.py twilightforest --strict
  ```

- **Deviation 1 (catalyst owner)** and **`block_face_sturdy`**: both are now expressible and are
  re-slice items, not gaps.
- **The dimension's terrain** and everything that stands on it: the two Java density types
  (`blocked(...)` above) are the wall, and the slice's chunk generator is deliberately empty. The
  in-game leg (leg 2 of `tools/e2e.sh`) is written but unrun: `horizon-launcher` is not installed
  here, so no game carrying this package can be built — `blocked(harness)`, not a claim.
